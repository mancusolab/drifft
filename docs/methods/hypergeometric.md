# Hypergeometric sampling

[`HypergeometricSampling`](../api/sampling.md#drifft.HypergeometricSampling) maps a final population distribution to a sample drawn
without replacement. It does not advance a generation or apply selection.

Configuration: [API reference](../api/sampling.md).

`pop_size` and `sample_size` count diploid individuals. The sampling law below
draws $n=2\,\mathtt{sample\_size}$ allele-bearing copies from
$M=2\,\mathtt{pop\_size}$ population copies without replacement.

## Problem and representation

For $M$ population gene copies and $n$ sampled copies, the observation matrix is

$$
S_{jk}=\frac{\binom{j}{k}\binom{M-j}{n-k}}{\binom{M}{n}},
\qquad j=0,\ldots,M,\quad k=0,\ldots,n.
$$

Its transpose maps population mass to sample mass. Multiplying sample values
by $S$ gives conditional expectations indexed by population count. Each column
is a polynomial of degree at most $n$ in the population count $j$.

## Computation

A full matrix costs $O(Mn)$ to store and apply. As in the FFT backend, local
polynomial interpolation replaces repeated kernel evaluations with a smaller
set of prepared rows. Unlike binomial reproduction, the sampling rows live on
integer population counts; a finite-difference identity controls their spacing.
For a stencil of $p$ representative counts,

$$
\widetilde S_{j,:}=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\ell_i(j)R_{x_i,:},
\qquad
\ell_i(j)=\prod_{k\ne i}\frac{j-x_k}{x_i-x_k}.
$$

The planner chooses integer nodes from an interpolation error bound, with exact
reflection across $M/2$. Representative probabilities are generated by ratios
outward from the hypergeometric mode. Small tails are discarded and the retained
rows are normalized during preparation.

`expect` evaluates the retained rows against sample values, then interpolates
those expectations at population counts. `advance` uses the matched transpose:
aggregate population mass at representatives, then scatter through their rows.
Both operations and their derivatives use fixed geometry and bounded batches,
following the streaming sampling pattern in the NUFFT backend.

## Differentiation

For fixed sampling geometry, let $\widetilde S$ denote the prepared approximation.
Population mass $p$ maps to sample mass $q=\widetilde S^T p$, while sample
values $f$ map to population expectations $g=\widetilde S f$. Their directional
derivatives and pullbacks are

$$
\dot q=\widetilde S^T\dot p,\qquad \bar p=\widetilde S\bar q,
\qquad
\dot g=\widetilde S\dot f,\qquad \bar f=\widetilde S^T\bar g.
$$

These are derivatives of the implemented approximation. Population and sample
sizes, interpolation nodes, and prepared weights are fixed configuration;
differentiate the input mass or values instead. The sampling maps themselves
have zero second derivatives, but nonlinear likelihoods composed with them
can have nonzero curvature. Conditioning on sample segregation also requires
differentiating its normalization denominator.

The `_CountInterpolation` HiJAX primitive supplies the linear JVP and its
streaming transpose. Reverse mode is derived with `vjp_from_lin`, retaining
interpolation geometry without retaining the input vector or a population-by-stencil
weight matrix. Other parts of a composed likelihood may still retain their
own residuals. Symbolically inactive tangents and cotangents skip interpolation;
numerically zero inputs remain differentiable. Tangent-only batching preserves
the shared primal output.

## Accuracy and controls

For $p\le n$, the forward difference of a hypergeometric row satisfies

$$
\Delta^p S_{j,:}=\frac{(n)_p}{(M)_p}(T-I)^p h^{M-p,n-p}_j,
$$

where $T$ shifts sample counts by one, $h$ is a hypergeometric row, and $(a)_p$
is a falling factorial. For largest grid gap $H$, this gives the planning bound

$$
\epsilon_{\mathrm{interp}}
\le \frac{2^{p-1}}{p}\frac{(n)_p}{(M)_p}H^p
$$

on half the row L1 error. Interpolation is exact when every integer count is
retained or $p>n$. Tail truncation is budgeted separately, including amplification
by the absolute sum of interpolation weights.

| Control | Role |
| --- | --- |
| `accuracy` | Combined interpolation and tail budget; zero uses uncompressed rows with full support |
| `interp_order` | Stencil size, with polynomial degree one less; default 8 |
| `tail_tol` | Optional stricter discarded mass per representative row; zero disables truncation |
| `batch_size` | Temporary interpolation workspace |

`interpolation_bound + tail_bound` bounds row half-L1 error in exact arithmetic.
For signed population mass $v$, output half-L1 error is at most that bound times
$\|v\|_1$. The uniform bound can be conservative and may select every population count.
Floating-point errors remain additional. Validate rare-bin relative
errors and conditional likelihoods separately.

!!! note "Derivation"

    The [interpolation analysis](https://github.com/mancusolab/drifft/blob/feat/hypergeometric-sampling/notes/hypergeometric_interpolation.md)
    derives the difference identity, integer-grid remainder, and tail amplification
    bound. This differs from fastDTWF's input-weighted TV grouping.

!!! warning "Signed approximation"

    Higher-order interpolation can produce small negative probabilities and mass
    outside a source count's exact support. Outputs are not clipped or normalized.
    Use `accuracy=0` for an uncompressed small reference. Before tail truncation,
    interpolation preserves conditional moments through degree `interp_order - 1`;
    preserving these moments alone does not establish distributional accuracy.

## Cost and reuse

For $R$ representative rows, maximum row width $W$, and stencil size $p$, stored
geometry costs $O(RW+Rp)$. Application costs $O(Mp+RW)$; the regular reflected
grid permits direct stencil lookup. Interpolation workspace is controlled by `batch_size`; neither application
nor its transpose retains a population-by-stencil matrix. The full population
input or output still has $M+1$ entries.

Sampling all copies is the identity; sampling zero copies returns total mass
at count zero. These cases need no stored rows. Construction runs on the host;
application uses JAX on the selected device. GPU execution has not yet been
validated. Direct sampling from Jacobi's reduced state is not implemented.

## Usage

```python
import jax
import jax.numpy as jnp
import drifft

jax.config.update("jax_enable_x64", True)
sampling = drifft.HypergeometricSampling(
    pop_size=64,
    sample_size=10,
    accuracy=1e-8,
)
population = jnp.zeros(sampling.source_size()).at[32].set(1.0)
sample = sampling.advance(population)

values = jnp.arange(sampling.dest_size()) / (2 * sampling.sample_size)
expected_frequency = population @ sampling.expect(values)
assert jnp.isclose(expected_frequency, 0.25, rtol=0, atol=1e-10)
```

For a history, pass `model.advance(initial).distribution` to `sampling.advance`.
To work backward, pass `sampling.expect(sample_values)` to `model.expect`;
matched demographic expectations require Sequential.

The operator accepts full count vectors, including endpoints. For a segregating
population spectrum, explicitly pad it with zeros at both ends. Sampling can
produce monomorphic observations even from a segregating population; condition
on sample segregation only after projection, if the observation scheme requires it.
