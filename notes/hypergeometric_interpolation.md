# Integer-grid interpolation of hypergeometric sampling rows

This note derives the exact-arithmetic planning bound used by
`HypergeometricSampling`. It is a bound on half the row L1 error, including
when the approximate row has negative entries. It excludes floating-point
errors in row construction, interpolation, and accumulation.

## A finite-difference identity

Let $h_j$ denote the vector of probabilities for $H_{M,j,n}$, padded with zeros
outside sample counts $0,\ldots,n$. Let $T$ shift a vector one position toward
higher sample counts, and let $\Delta$ take a forward difference in $j$.
For $p\le n$ and $0\le j\le M-p$,

$$
\Delta^p h_j
=\frac{(n)_p}{(M)_p}(T-I)^p h^{M-p,n-p}_j,
$$

where $(a)_p=a(a-1)\cdots(a-p+1)$.

To see this, designate $p$ initially ancestral population copies and take the
mixed difference obtained by turning each into a derived copy. If any designated
copy is absent from the sample, its difference is zero. All $p$ are sampled
with probability $(n)_p/(M)_p$. Conditional on this event, the remaining sample
has distribution $H_{M-p,j,n-p}$ and each toggle applies $T-I$. Exchangeability
identifies the mixed difference with $\Delta^p$ in the success count.
Consequently,

$$
\|\Delta^p h_j\|_1\le 2^p\frac{(n)_p}{(M)_p}.
$$

For $p>n$, the difference is zero: each row entry is a polynomial of degree at
most $n$ in $j$, equivalently a product of two falling-factorial polynomials.

## From differences to interpolation error

For any $p+1$ distinct integer nodes, their divided difference is a convex
combination of divided differences on $p+1$ consecutive integers within their
span. This follows by repeatedly inserting missing integer knots. If
$t_0<\cdots<t_p$ and $u$ lies strictly between the endpoints and is not a node,

$$
f[t_0,\ldots,t_p]
=\frac{u-t_0}{t_p-t_0}f[t_0,\ldots,t_{p-1},u]
+\frac{t_p-u}{t_p-t_0}f[t_1,\ldots,t_p,u].
$$

Each new span is shorter, the weights are nonnegative and sum to one, and
divided differences are symmetric in their nodes. At consecutive integer
knots, the divided difference is $\Delta^p f/p!$. Thus the vector divided
difference of $h$ has L1 norm at most $2^p(n)_p/((M)_p p!)$.

Interpolating $h$ at $p$ integer nodes $x_0<\cdots<x_{p-1}$ gives, for another
integer count $j$ inside their span,

$$
\frac12\|h_j-I_ph(j)\|_1
\le\frac{2^{p-1}}{p!}\frac{(n)_p}{(M)_p}
\prod_{i=0}^{p-1}|j-x_i|.
$$

If the largest node gap is $H$, and $j$ lies in cell $s$, the product is bounded
by $H^p(s+1)!(p-1-s)!\le H^p(p-1)!$. Therefore the planner uses

$$
\epsilon_{\mathrm{interp}}
\le \frac{2^{p-1}}p\frac{(n)_p}{(M)_p}H^p.
$$

The bound is zero at retained nodes, when every population count is retained,
or when $p>n$. Integer nodes are `floor(i*M/L)` on the left half, reflected exactly on the
right. An odd interval count $L$ avoids an unpaired midpoint. The planner checks
the actual largest gap after reflection and refines until the bound is at most
half the requested `accuracy`.

## Truncation and amplification

For each representative row, retain the exact support intersected with an
outward-rounded window of radius $\sqrt{(n/2)\log(2/\delta)}$ around its mean.
Hoeffding's without-replacement inequality bounds omitted mass by $\delta$.
Normalizing the retained row changes it by at most $\delta$ in half-L1 distance.

Polynomial interpolation has signed weights. If their absolute sum is bounded
by $\Lambda$, the resulting tail error is at most $\Lambda\delta$.
For minimum and maximum grid gaps $h,H$, a conservative bound is

$$
\Lambda\le\left(\frac Hh\right)^{p-1}
\max_{0\le s<p-1}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}
\frac{\prod_{k\ne i}\max(|s-k|,|s+1-k|)}{i!(p-1-i)!}.
$$

This follows by bounding the numerator of every Lagrange weight over its cell
and its denominator using the minimum gap. Full integer grids have $\Lambda=1$.
Choose $\delta\le\mathtt{accuracy}/(2\Lambda)$. Interpolation and tail bounds
then sum to at most `accuracy` in exact arithmetic. `tail_tol` can lower
$\delta$ further; zero disables truncation.

For signed input mass $v$, the same bound gives
$\frac12\|\widetilde S^Tv-S^Tv\|_1\le\mathtt{accuracy}\|v\|_1$.
This is an absolute error statement, not a relative error guarantee for rare
bins or conditional likelihoods. Higher-order interpolation can produce
negative entries and does not preserve each row's exact support. No clipping
or output normalization is applied.

## Implementation and validation

Rows are generated by probability ratios outward from the hypergeometric mode,
then normalized. Local barycentric interpolation uses normalized coordinates.
It is evaluated in batches and has an explicit streaming transpose, following
the fixed-geometry sampling pattern in the NUFFT backend. No population-by-stencil
matrix is retained by either action or its derivative.

Tests check the difference identity against exact integer combinatorics,
integer-grid interpolation bounds against independently computed hypergeometric
rows, the absolute-weight bound, exact polynomial cases, and matched JAX
transformations. The derivation above is specific to this implementation;
it is not an inherited error guarantee from fastDTWF.

Background: [DLMF interpolation and divided differences](https://dlmf.nist.gov/3.3)
and [Spence et al. (2023), Appendix A](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10627256/)
for hypergeometric tail bounds and the separate TV-compression approach.
